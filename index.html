01Executive Summary

Five strategic priorities, five answers.

Five priorities, restated plainly, each with a direct answer. Every figure here is traceable to source.

1

Build a three-layer operating system. Today the company runs four separate systems (SAP BI for finance, MAssist for sales, Qandle for HR, a CRM for customer care) with uneven use and no single live view. Pull them onto one Tier-1 ERP at the base. Add an AI-driven MIS with daily auto-reports on top, which the company has none of today. Run an S&OP rhythm on a four-warehouse network. Cost runs 1.5 to 2.5% of revenue over seven years. Go-live April 2027, which gives two clean audited years before the DRHP.

Behind these sit eleven adjacent extensions and nine contrarian pilots.
3
The IPO-Ready Priority Build a robust organisation for an IPO in three years: team structure, the right hires, BD in India and abroad, product development, and a full restructuring led by an incoming CEO.

02Diagnosis

Why growth has slowed. The evidence.

Five symptoms, each verified, and several now confirmed by management's own answers to our questions. The plan fixes them in order of leverage, not in order of how obvious they look.

Symptom01

Recent growth has slowed and trails the category.

Management reports a 10 to 15% CAGR over the last five years. But the most recent year (FY25) slowed to about 4.4% (₹283 cr against ₹271 cr), while the category is growing 12 to 18% (Ken Research) and the hair-dryer market 8.1% a year. So Vega has a solid five-year record but a recent year well below its own market. Management's own first pain point says the same thing: growth has not kept up with the opportunity in the market and the channels. Volumes need to re-accelerate, and the Yamuna plant needs them to.1

Symptom02

Profit fell while debt tripled.

PAT was down 11.38% in FY25. Borrowings were up 203.88%. Live bank charges total ₹50.30 cr, all with one bank. Debt-to-equity went from under 0.05× to 0.21× in a single year. Networth rose 12.39% (around ₹238 cr) and held the ratios together, but the direction is profit shrinking, not profit building. A ₹48 cr term loan was taken in June 2025, just after the year closed, so FY26 interest costs rise sharply.2

Symptom03

The leadership team is not yet built for an IPO.

There is a CEO, with seven to eight functional heads under them, reporting to the Managing Director. So the basic structure exists. What is missing is the IPO-grade layer: a CFO who has taken a company public, a qualified Company Secretary, an Internal Auditor, and clearer depth in finance, supply chain and product. Management's own second pain point names it directly: gaps in execution, accountability and ownership across functions. Building that layer is the highest-value fix in this plan.3

Symptom04

Competitors are closing in on Vega's prices and channels.

Havells did ₹21,778 cr in FY25, 77 times Vega's size, and is pushing harder into personal care. Philips holds 20% of the hair-dryer market. The Agaro / UCL group hit ₹1,910 cr, mostly D2C. Lifelong did ₹540 cr, mostly on Amazon. Bombay Shaving Company did ₹271 cr, close to Vega's size, is filing for an IPO in 18 to 24 months, and is moving into appliances. Vega's 10% hair-dryer share is squeezed from above by Philips and Dyson, and from below by Kemei, Nova, Lifelong and BSC.4

Symptom05

The KnitPro group overlap is an IPO landmine if left alone.

The CMD runs both Vega and KnitPro International. Group entities share directors and addresses with Vega Industries, and the KnitPro trademark sits inside the group. Related-party flows are an estimated ₹30 to 75 cr a year, 10 to 26% of revenue, above the 10% SEBI threshold that triggers scrutiny. This has to be cleaned up before the DRHP through a transfer-pricing study, arm's-length contracts, and Audit Committee oversight.5

The Point Most People Miss
Anyone can read symptoms 1, 2 and 4 off public filings. Symptoms 3 and 5 are invisible from outside today, but they are exactly what a Big-4 IPO advisor flags in week one. Management's own three pain points (growth, execution and accountability, and weak data-driven decisions) line up almost exactly with this list. The plan fixes the hidden ones first.
03Pillar 01 · The Operating System

From founder judgement to operating rhythm.

Right now the company runs on the founder's judgement. An IPO-bound company runs on rhythm. Judgement only scales as far as one person's hours. Rhythm scales without limit. This pillar builds that rhythm in three layers (ERP, AI-MIS, supply chain) and times the ERP go-live to land before the audit window the DRHP needs.

Layer 01 · ERP Backbone

The company is not starting from nothing. It already runs four separate systems: SAP BI for finance, MAssist for sales, Qandle for HR, and a CRM for customer care. The problem is that they are separate, used unevenly, and give no single live picture of the business. The job is to consolidate onto one ERP. Five platforms were compared: SAP S/4 HANA Cloud RISE, Oracle Fusion Cloud, Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central, Infor M3, and Odoo Enterprise. Each was scored on eight weighted criteria against Vega's profile: ₹283 cr heading to ₹1,000 cr, 700-plus products, light assembly, many channels, IPO in 2029.

Layer 03 · The Customer Tool: "Vega HairPrint"

A simple online quiz that recommends the right Vega products for a person's hair. It takes about a minute. It does three jobs at once: it lifts sales on the website, it gives salons a tool to use with clients, and it tells the product team exactly what people need (especially for Indian hair and Indian weather).

HairPrint · The 60-Second Quiz

Step 1, Your Hair: type (straight, wavy, curly, coily, with photo guides), texture, length, thickness.

Step 2, Your Situation: scalp condition, main concern (frizz, heat damage, breakage, thinning, dandruff, dryness, hard-water build-up), age, climate, hard-water exposure (worked out from the pincode).

Step 3, Your Routine: how often you style, comfort with heat tools, time you have, budget, whether it is a gift.

Result: a four-step routine (Tool, Care, Style, Maintain) with specific Vega products, a match score, and a plain reason for each pick. If the match is weak, it offers a WhatsApp chat with a Vega stylist, which also captures a salon lead.

Built on standard cloud AI tools. Build cost ₹1.2 to 2.0 cr, running cost ₹50 to 80 L a year.7

The Long-Horizon Bet · Marine Ingredients + Ayurveda Skincare

This is the biggest idea in the plan, and the one that needs the most patience. India has two things almost no other country has together: a very long coastline now officially measured at 11,099 km, and the deepest botanical tradition in the world. Put marine ingredients (seaweed, algae, marine collagen, sea minerals) together with Indian botanicals (ashwagandha, neem, turmeric, aloe vera, brahmi, moringa) and you get a skincare category that is genuinely hard for a Western or Korean brand to copy. The government is already funding the raw-material base: a ₹640 cr national seaweed mission, a Multipurpose Seaweed Park in Tamil Nadu, and a target to grow seaweed output many times over.17

Why this fits Vega, and what to be honest about

The fit: Vega is already moving into cosmetics (through the group's Sery brand) and into salons (through Vega Professional). Marine-plus-Ayurveda skincare rides both of those channels. It carries far higher margins than selling raw seaweed, and it gives the brand a story no competitor can easily match: ingredients from India's own seas and soil, made for Indian skin and hair.

How to run it: as a chemistry and formulation business, not a hardware one. It runs through the group's cosmetics brand, the salon channel, and outside contract manufacturers who already make to global standard. Vega does not need to build a chemical plant. This keeps it light on capital and away from the factory's core skills.

The honest part: this is a 5-year-plus build, not a quick win. Today India sells mostly raw seaweed, not finished marine skincare; the premium formulation lead still sits with France, South Korea and Norway. So the right claim is "early mover in marine-plus-Ayurveda," not "world first." A realistic 3-year revenue band is ₹15 to 60 cr. Treat it as a high-value differentiation and brand bet, deliberately placed behind the nearer-term tools and cosmetics moves.

Even as a longer play, it lifts the whole equity story. It positions Vega not as a hair-tools company but as an India-rooted beauty platform spanning tools, cosmetics, and science-led skincare. The supporting markets are real and growing: India's herbal cosmetic ingredients are heading past ₹21,000 cr by 2033 (10.6% a year), dermatology-grade skincare is growing over 10% a year, and the salon market is on track to roughly double to over ₹1.9 lakh cr by 2033.18

05Pillar 03 · IPO-Ready Organisation

From founder to institution.

An IPO is not really a finance event. It is an organisation event with a finance date attached. Three years out means: 12 months to lock the CEO, CFO and Company Secretary, then 12 months to fill the rest of the C-suite and the board, then 12 months to run the company as an institution before filing. Senior FMCG talent is unusually available right now, and that window closes inside 12 months.

Move A · Set the CEO Mandate for the IPO by November 2026

A CEO already runs the company, reporting to the Managing Director, with seven to eight functional heads underneath. The question is not whether there is a CEO, but whether the role is set up to lead a public company. The CEO mandate must clearly own the P&L, all hiring below the CMD, every process, every target, the ERP rollout, and the IPO programme, so the CMD can move up to Executive Chairman: running the board, setting direction, holding investor relationships, and backing the IPO. If the current CEO is the right person to take the company public, back them fully and clear the mandate. If not, the time to bring in an IPO-experienced CEO is now, while senior talent is unusually available.

The IPO-Grade CEO Standard

18 to 22 years in consumer goods or appliances. Has run a ₹500 to 1,500 cr P&L. IPO experience strongly preferred. Comfortable working under a founder-Chairman. When a market is consolidating, defending what you own is the best form of attack. Vega has three strongholds and five main competitors closing in. For each one, the plan picks a fight Vega can win, using an advantage the competitor cannot copy, never by trying to outspend a much bigger company.
